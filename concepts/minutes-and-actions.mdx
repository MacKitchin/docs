Minutes are the official written record of what happened in a meeting. In ConnectMeetings, the minutes editor opens automatically when a meeting moves to In Progress and is pre-populated with the approved agenda structure — so organizers spend less time formatting and more time capturing the substance of discussions, decisions, and votes. Action items created during or after the meeting are linked to the minutes record and tracked separately until they are complete.

## Taking Minutes

When a meeting starts, ConnectMeetings activates the live minutes editor for the meeting's organizer and any co-editors. The editor mirrors the agenda structure: each agenda item appears as a section, ready for notes.

Multiple organizers can write in the same minutes document simultaneously. Changes appear in real time for all active editors, similar to collaborative document editing in Microsoft Word Online. Every keystroke is saved automatically — there is no Save button, and no risk of losing work.

Key features of the minutes editor include:

- **Agenda-synced sections** — One section per agenda item, in agenda order, with the item title and presenter pre-filled.
- **Speaker log** — Record who spoke and a summary of their contribution within each agenda item section.
- **Inline motions** — Trigger the motion recorder from within any agenda item section (see below).
- **Timestamps** — Each section records the time it was opened, giving a precise timeline of the meeting.

## Recording Governance Events

ConnectMeetings provides structured forms for the governance events most critical to the public record.

### Motions

To record a motion, select **Add Motion** within the relevant agenda item section. The motion form captures:

- **Motion text** — The exact wording of the proposed motion.
- **Moved by** — Selected from the attendee list.
- **Seconded by** — Selected from the attendee list.
- **Discussion notes** — A free-text field for summarizing debate.
- **Outcome** — Carried, Defeated, Tabled, or Withdrawn.

### Roll Call Votes

When a recorded vote is required, select **Add Roll Call Vote**. ConnectMeetings displays the full attendee list and lets the secretary record each member's vote — **For**, **Against**, or **Abstain** — individually. The final tally is calculated automatically and appended to the motion record.

### Resolutions

Resolutions passed by motion are automatically numbered sequentially and included in the minutes export. You can view all resolutions from a meeting under **Minutes → Resolutions**.

## Action Items

Action items represent commitments made during or following a meeting. Each action item contains:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Title | A clear, one-line description of the task |
| Owner | The person responsible for completing the action |
| Due date | The target completion date |
| Source | The meeting and agenda item where the action was created |
| Priority | Urgency level — Low, Medium, High, or Critical — used to sort and filter the action item dashboard |
| Status | Current progress state (see lifecycle below) |
| Notes | Optional detail or context for the owner |

### Action Item Lifecycle

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open">
    The action item has been created and assigned to an owner. The owner receives an email notification with the task details and due date. The item appears on the owner's action item dashboard.
  </Step>
  <Step title="In Progress">
    The owner has acknowledged the action and marked it as underway. Organizers and admins can see that work has begun. The owner can add progress notes at any time.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete">
    The owner has marked the action as done. Organizers can review and confirm completion. Completed actions remain linked to the originating meeting for the record.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Archived">
    Completed action items are archived when the meeting is archived. Archived items are read-only but remain searchable and appear in reports.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Minutes Approval

Like agendas, minutes follow an approval workflow before they become the official record.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Draft">
    Minutes are being written or reviewed internally. Only organizers and admins can view Draft minutes. The organizer can continue editing until the record is ready for formal review.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submitted">
    The organizer submits the completed minutes to the designated approver — typically the meeting chair or a senior administrator. The approver receives an email notification with a direct link to the review view.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Approved or Rejected">
    The approver reviews the minutes and takes one of two actions. **Approved** locks the minutes as the official record and notifies all participants. **Rejected** returns the minutes to Draft with comments so the organizer can make corrections and resubmit.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Distributed">
    Approved minutes are published to the meeting record and automatically distributed to all participants by email. If your organization has enabled the public portal, the approved minutes are published there as well.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Once minutes are approved, they are locked. Corrections must follow the formal amendment process — typically a motion at the next meeting — rather than being edited directly. This protects the integrity of the record.

## Exporting Records

ConnectMeetings provides two export formats for completed meeting records.

- **PDF export** — Produces a formatted, print-ready PDF of the approved minutes, including all motions, votes, resolutions, and attendee records. Access it from **Minutes → Export → PDF**.
- **Action item CSV export** — Exports all action items from a meeting (or across multiple meetings) as a spreadsheet, including owner, due date, and current status. Access it from **Action Items → Export → CSV**.

Both exports include a metadata header showing the meeting name, date, and the name of the approver who certified the record.

<Tip>
  Assign action items during the meeting rather than after. When an action is created in the live minutes editor, the owner is notified immediately and the due date is visible to everyone in the room. This real-time accountability reduces the number of items that fall through the cracks between the meeting and the follow-up review.
</Tip>
