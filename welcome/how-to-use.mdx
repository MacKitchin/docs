Use the Company Brain to **find the best available answer and its owner**, not to bypass the system that owns the underlying fact. The site is written for internal staff and approved internal agents.

## The ten-minute orientation

<Steps>
  <Step title="Understand the business">
    Read [Company overview](/company/overview), [How the business works](/company/business-model), and [Brands](/company/brands). These explain the supplier-and-hosted-buyer economy behind the rest of the site.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Learn where event facts live">
    Use the [Event calendar](/events/calendar/overview) for dates, venues, formats, eligibility, appointment counts, and cancellation information. Event pages preserve discrepancies rather than guessing which version is correct. Public homepages, registration, maps, and decks live on [Public event sites](/events/public-sites) and are Working marketing pointers, not Canonical event facts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Learn how customer questions are handled">
    [Exhibitor Support](/exhibitor-support/overview) and [Hosted Buyer Support](/hosted-buyer-support/overview) are internal playbooks. They combine staff context, safe response wording, and escalation boundaries; they are not customer handbooks.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find people and ownership">
    [People & Ownership](/people/overview) explains the organisation. [Who owns what](/people/who-owns-what) is the routing table for questions, decisions, and escalation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Know which source wins">
    Read [Source of truth](/reference/source-of-truth), then [Known issues](/reference/known-issues). Check both before changing a disputed fact or treating a Working source as policy.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## When the answer will be customer-facing

<Steps>
  <Step title="Identify the audience and event">
    Establish whether the person is an exhibitor, supplier prospect, hosted-buyer applicant, approved hosted buyer, attendee, or another audience. Identify the relevant event and product year.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Retrieve the event fact">
    Check the Event calendar. Do not reconstruct a date, venue, appointment count, or policy from memory, email, or a project page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use the internal playbook">
    Open the matching support page. Use its response guidance as the basis for a concise customer reply; do not forward the full internal page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply the boundary">
    Escalate billing, invoices, contracts, legal terms, complaints, specific discounts, unpublished prices, and named attendee lists. If sources conflict, report both versions internally and route the discrepancy to the owner.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  A page being readable does not make every detail customer-safe. Internal rationale, system fields, data defects, owner gaps, and escalation notes stay internal.
</Warning>

## Source and status conventions

| Convention | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Canonical** | The registered source that owns the fact. |
| **Working** | Useful operational material that is not yet authoritative. Disclose the status when it matters. |
| **Deprecated** | Retained for history or migration only. Do not present it as current. |
| Source date | When a time-sensitive source was captured or reviewed. It is not a promise that nothing has changed since. |
| Known discrepancy | Two sources disagree. Keep both versions and ask the owner to reconcile them. |
| `{/* TODO: ... */}` | A fact is unconfirmed. Do not fill it from inference. |

## What this site does not contain

The Company Brain does not hold record-level customer data, named deal values, attendee rosters, customer contact lists, credentials, or secrets. Those remain in access-controlled systems. This site documents definitions, ownership, process, safeguards, and source locations.

## If a page is wrong or incomplete

1. Check the owning source in [Source of truth](/reference/source-of-truth).
2. Check [Known issues](/reference/known-issues) for an already logged discrepancy.
3. Capture the conflicting versions, source URLs, and review date.
4. Route the decision to the owner in [Who owns what](/people/who-owns-what).
5. Update the Company Brain only after the owning source or a documented decision supports the change.
