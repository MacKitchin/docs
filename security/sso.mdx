Single Sign-On (SSO) lets your team access ConnectMeetings using the same Microsoft work account they already use every day — no separate password to remember, reset, or manage. For IT administrators, SSO means tighter control over who can access ConnectMeetings, automatic de-provisioning when a staff member leaves, and a reduced attack surface with one fewer credential set to protect.

## Supported SSO Provider

ConnectMeetings supports **Microsoft 365 SSO** via **Azure Active Directory / Microsoft Entra ID**. SSO is available on all ConnectMeetings plans, including Starter.

<Info>
  If your organization uses a different identity provider such as Okta, Google Workspace, or ADFS, contact [support@connectmeetings.com](mailto:support@connectmeetings.com) to discuss your requirements. Microsoft 365 is the only natively supported SSO provider at this time.
</Info>

## How It Works

When SSO is enabled, the sign-in experience is straightforward for your team:

1. A user visits the ConnectMeetings login page and clicks **Sign in with Microsoft**.
2. They authenticate with their Microsoft work account — using their organization's existing MFA policy if one is in place.
3. On their first sign-in, ConnectMeetings automatically creates their ConnectMeetings user account. No manual provisioning is needed.
4. On subsequent sign-ins, they are logged straight into ConnectMeetings.

Your organization's Microsoft tenant policies — including conditional access rules and MFA requirements — apply automatically.

## Setting Up SSO for Your Organization

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open SSO Settings">
    In ConnectMeetings, navigate to **Settings → Security → Single Sign-On**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enable Microsoft 365 SSO">
    Click **Enable Microsoft 365 SSO**. A configuration panel will expand below the button.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter Your Microsoft Tenant Domain">
    Enter your organization's Microsoft tenant domain — for example, `yourorg.onmicrosoft.com` or your custom domain if one is configured in Entra ID. This restricts SSO sign-ins to users within your tenant.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Authorize in Microsoft Entra ID">
    A Microsoft admin must sign in and grant ConnectMeetings consent in Microsoft Entra ID. Click **Authorize with Microsoft** and complete the authorization as a Global Admin or Application Admin. This step only needs to be done once.
  </Step>
  <Step title="SSO Is Now Active">
    Once authorization is complete, the **Sign in with Microsoft** button appears on your organization's ConnectMeetings login page. Users can now sign in with their Microsoft work accounts.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Auto-Provisioning

You do not need to create ConnectMeetings accounts for new staff members manually. When SSO is active, any user from your Microsoft tenant who signs in for the first time is automatically provisioned with a ConnectMeetings account.

New users are assigned the **Member** role by default. Admins can adjust their role and permissions from **Settings → Users** after their first sign-in.

## Enforcing SSO

By default, SSO is an additional sign-in option — users can still log in with their ConnectMeetings email and password. To require all users to sign in exclusively through Microsoft SSO:

1. Go to **Settings → Security → Single Sign-On**.
2. Enable the **Require SSO for all users** toggle.
3. Click **Save**.

When SSO enforcement is active, password-based login is disabled for all users in your organization.

<Warning>
  Enabling SSO enforcement will immediately log out any users who currently sign in with a password only. Make sure all users have a Microsoft 365 work account in your tenant before enforcing SSO. Users without a valid account in your Microsoft tenant will lose access to ConnectMeetings.
</Warning>

<Note>
  SSO enforcement requires all users in your organization to have an active Microsoft 365 account in the tenant you have configured. If some users do not have Microsoft 365 accounts, do not enable enforcement — those users will be unable to log in.
</Note>

## Fallback Login

If Microsoft's services experience an outage, ConnectMeetings provides a fallback mechanism so your team is not locked out.

- If SSO is enabled but **not enforced**, users can still sign in with their ConnectMeetings email and password during a Microsoft outage.
- If SSO is **enforced**, admins can temporarily disable enforcement from **Settings → Security → Single Sign-On** to restore password-based login access during an outage.

We recommend that at least one Admin account retains a ConnectMeetings password as a recovery option, even when SSO enforcement is active.
