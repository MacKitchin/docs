The application is short, but qualification is substantive. This page explains the staff view of both.

## The application

<Steps>
  <Step title="Submit the application">
    The application takes about **ten minutes** to complete. The hosted buyer provides company details, role, event portfolio, sourcing scale, and preferred track.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pay the refundable deposit">
    A **\$200 refundable deposit** secures the application. Current guidance says it is returned when the assigned appointment schedule is completed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Hosted Buyer Experience Team review">
    The team vets the hosted buyer's application against track criteria: role, sourcing volume, event history, rotation capacity.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pairing with a dedicated contact">
    Approved hosted buyers are paired with a dedicated point of contact from the Hosted Buyer Experience Team who supports the hosted buyer through pre-event, onsite, and follow-up.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What Connect verifies

- **Role** and decision authority for sourcing
- **Event portfolio**: two-year verifiable history
- **Sourcing scale**: rooms on peak, budget size, or attendance depending on track
- **Rotation**: national or regional as appropriate to the event

Providing accurate information is important. Misrepresenting scale or authority is grounds for disqualification and can affect future eligibility across the Connect portfolio.

## The deposit

<Info>
  The \$200 deposit is described as **refundable in full** when the assigned appointment schedule is completed. It is not presented as an event fee.
</Info>

The deposit is the mechanism that keeps the marketplace commercially viable. Suppliers pay for a specific number of appointments, and those appointments only have value if hosted buyers actually show up. From the [business model page](/company/business-model):

> The deposit is the enforcement mechanism for the whole model. If hosted buyers skip appointments, suppliers do not renew, and renewals are 79 percent of supplier revenue.

### When the deposit is forfeited

- **No-shows** at scheduled appointments
- **Late cancellation** inside the hosted buyer cancellation window (typically inside 7 days of the event, plus a one-night hotel fee)
- **Failure to complete** the assigned appointment schedule

<Warning>
  Two hosted-buyer cancellation policies are published across the portfolio: one on the Hosted Buyers page and one in the Terms & Conditions. Always confirm the event and registrant type before assuming that the general 30-day cutoff applies.
</Warning>

### When the deposit is returned

- **Full refund or credit** is described for cancellation **30 or more days** before the event
- **Full refund** on completing the hosted buyer's assigned appointment schedule onsite

## The appointment commitment

A hosted buyer commits to a **full appointment schedule** matched to documented sourcing needs. At the flagship this can be **up to 45 appointments** across the event. Regional schedules are usually shorter.

Appointments are:

- **One-to-one** with a supplier representative
- **Short and focused** (some events describe them as nine minutes)
- **Matched in advance** from mutual-interest requests, often AI-assisted
- **Tracked onsite**; missed appointments count against the hosted buyer's deposit

See [Onsite experience](/hosted-buyer-support/onsite-experience) for how the day is structured around these blocks.

## After approval

Once approved and paired with a Hosted Buyer Experience contact, the hosted buyer moves into the pre-event phase: submitting supplier requests, arranging travel, and preparing a sourcing brief. See [Preparing for the event](/hosted-buyer-support/preparing-for-the-event).
