## The shape of a Connect event

Nearly every event follows the same three-beat structure, which is worth internalising because it drives the whole operational calendar.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Day one: arrive and open">
    Regiception (combined registration and reception), a first-timer orientation at larger events, a welcome education session, a first block of marketplace appointments, and an opening reception hosted by the destination.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Day two: the working day">
    Breakfast and education, a morning appointment block, lunch and learn, an afternoon appointment block, then an open night in the host city. This is where most of the business gets done.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Day three: close out">
    Grab-and-go breakfast, a final appointment block, and departures. Flagships add a closing concert or the annual Connect 5K.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Two-day regional events compress this into days one and two. Flagship events extend it with tradeshow experiences, speaker showcases, hosted meals, and a rebooking lounge.

## Recurring operational deadlines

| Deadline type | Pattern | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supplier hotel room block closes | 3-4 weeks before the event | Connect Marketplace 3 Aug; Connect Texas 5 Oct; Connect West 14 Oct; Connect South 16 Oct; Winter Marketplace 27 Oct |
| Standard cancellation cutoff | 57 days before | Refund minus 10% administration charge; nothing refunded inside 56 days |
| Hosted buyer cancellation cutoff | 30 days before | Full refund or credit at 30\+ days; inside 7 days, no refund plus one hotel night fee |
| Hosted buyer deposit | \$200, refundable | Returned on completing the assigned appointment schedule |

<Warning>
  Two different cancellation policies are published on nearly every event site - one on the Hosted Buyers page, one in the Terms & Conditions. They almost certainly apply to different registrant types, but no site states that explicitly. Never quote one without checking which registrant you are talking to. Logged on [Known issues](/governance/known-issues).
</Warning>

## Standing commitments across all events

- **Sustainability**: reusable water bottles via Fill it Forward, onsite recycling, eco-friendly and locally sourced food, food donation to local charities, quiet and prayer rooms, recyclable signage, carpet reuse at flagships.
- **Code of conduct**: zero tolerance for harassment, discrimination, or violence at any live or digital touchpoint, covering attendees, speakers, sponsors, exhibitors, and staff.
- **Governing law**: New York, per Terms & Conditions, regardless of where the event is held.
- **Media partners**: BizBash, TSNN, and Corporate Event News appear across the portfolio.

## How events appear in Salesforce

| Concept | Object or field |
| --- | --- |
| A specific event instance | `Marketplace__c` - roughly 10 to 15 per year, with `Event_Type__c`, `Start_Date__c`, `End_Date__c`, `Event_Status__c` |
| A hosted buyer registration | `Registration__c`, linked to Contact, Account, Campaign, and `Marketplace__c` |
| Registration lifecycle | `Registration_Stage__c`: Registering, Pending Confirmation, Registered, Fully Registered, Cancelled, Declined, Verbal, Stopped |
| Event revenue attribution | `OpportunityLineItem.Event_Name__c` and boolean flags such as `IsSpringMarketplaceProduct__c` |
| Registration counts | Rollup fields on `Marketplace__c` such as `Fully_Registered_Count__c` |

<Tip>
  **Fully Registered** is the only stage that means confirmed. Registering and Pending Confirmation are not attendance.
</Tip>
