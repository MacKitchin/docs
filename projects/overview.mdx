## How they fit together

These are not four independent efforts. They form a single chain, and the order matters.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Fix the container - Company OS">
    You cannot ground an agent on a workspace with three competing front doors and 35 scattered databases. The Notion rebuild establishes one canonical home per domain, recorded in a source-of-truth registry. Everything downstream depends on it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fix the data - Data Consolidation">
    Connect and BizBash account, contact, lead, and opportunity data has never been merged. Routing, reporting, and coverage all break on it. Until this lands, any agent answering a question about account ownership is answering from a broken dataset.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Extract the knowledge - Supplier Knowledge Intelligence">
    The real answers live in reps' inboxes, not in documentation. Mining 26,600 supplier emails turns tribal knowledge into a governed, citable FAQ and article library that agents can ground on.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Deploy the agents - Agentforce Rollout">
    With a clean container, clean data, and a grounded knowledge base, the agent roster goes live against a prioritised roadmap worth roughly 1,405 hours a year.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The through-line

Read the four projects together and one theme emerges: **this business runs on knowledge that has never been written down**. Loss reasons live in nobody's head because `Reason_Lost__c` is blank on 421 of 424 lost deals. Account ownership is unrecorded for 20,876 advertiser accounts. Event facts contradict themselves across a dozen websites. The projects are all, in different ways, an attempt to move institutional knowledge out of inboxes and individual memory and into systems that a person or an agent can query.
