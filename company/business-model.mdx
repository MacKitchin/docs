The commercial model is a reverse tradeshow: suppliers pay for scheduled appointments with hosted buyers. This page explains how that makes money.

## The core mechanic

A traditional tradeshow sells floor space and hopes buyers walk by. We invert it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Recruit and qualify buyers">
    Event planners apply to the hosted buyer programme. Qualification is real: a typical bar is generating 50 or more rooms on peak nights, ability to rotate destinations nationally or regionally, and a verifiable two-year event history. Applications take about ten minutes and require a refundable deposit, usually \$200.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sponsor their attendance">
    Qualified hosted buyers get roundtrip airfare, hotel for the event nights, meals, all networking events, and the education programme, at no cost. In exchange they commit to a full appointment schedule.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sell suppliers access to that room">
    Suppliers buy tiered packages priced by appointment count. At the flagship these run from Ice Breaker at \$4,250 for 12 appointments up to Deal Closer at \$5,950 for 40 appointments with guaranteed top-five requests.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Match and schedule before anyone travels">
    Appointments are matched, often AI-assisted, and scheduled in advance. Nobody arrives hoping for a conversation. Suppliers arrive with a diary.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Return the deposit, and rebook">
    The buyer deposit is refunded on completing the assigned appointments, and forfeited on no-shows. Rebooking for next year happens onsite in a dedicated rebooking lounge.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  The deposit is the enforcement mechanism for the whole model. If hosted buyers skip appointments, suppliers do not renew, and renewals are 79 percent of supplier revenue. Attendance compliance is a commercial control, not an admin detail.
</Warning>

## Where the rest of the revenue comes from

## Track qualification

Marketplace tracks are how we keep the room valuable. Each has its own bar, on top of the general minimum of 50 or more peak rooms, national rotation ability, and a two-year verifiable history.

| Track | Scale | Qualification highlights |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Corporate | 400\+ corporate event professionals | Director, VP, or C-suite; six to seven figure budgets |
| Association | 300\+ association event professionals | 50\+ rooms on peak |
| Citywide | - | 87% manage \$1M\+ budgets; 1,000\+ rooms or 100,000\+ sq ft minimum |
| Specialty | 300\+ specialty professionals | Social, military, and fraternal group planners |
| Sports | 200\+ sports event professionals | Competitive sporting events and sports-themed meetings |
| International (RTO) | 150\+ international tourism professionals | Receptive tour operators booking inbound North America travel; up to \$600 flight stipend per company |
| Business Travel | - | 1 in 3 buyers controls \$10M\+ travel budgets; 80%\+ manager level or above |
| Diversity | 100\+ target community | Faith-based, fraternal, nonprofit, education, LGBTQ\+, multicultural, and women's group planners |
| Informa | - | Informa PLC colleagues only; registration fees waived |

From the flagship Why Sponsor page: 61 percent of hosted buyers manage budgets over \$250K, 31 percent manage \$500K or more, 34 percent generate 1,000 or more room nights per event, 45 percent require 250 or more peak rooms, and 22 percent require 500 or more.
