Recurring meetings — council sessions, school board meetings, standing committee reviews — share a predictable structure. Agenda templates let you define that structure once and apply it every time, so you spend less time rebuilding the same items from scratch and more time focusing on the content that changes.

## What's in a template

A template is a reusable agenda blueprint. Each template can include:

- **Pre-defined agenda items** with fixed titles and types (e.g., Call to Order, Approval of Minutes, Adjournment)
- **Default presenters** assigned to specific items (e.g., the Chair always presents the Call to Order)
- **Standard time allocations** so meetings start with a realistic schedule built in
- **Placeholder descriptions** that prompt organizers to fill in meeting-specific details before publishing

Templates do not hold live meeting data. They are a starting point — you customize the populated items for each individual meeting after applying the template.

## Creating a template

You can create a template from scratch or save an existing agenda as a template.

**From scratch:** Go to **Settings → Templates → New Template**. Give the template a name, add your agenda items, and click **Save Template**.

**From an existing agenda:** Open any saved agenda draft, click the **More options (⋯)** menu, and select **Save as Template**. ConnectMeetings copies the current item structure — including titles, types, presenters, and time allocations — into a new reusable template.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the source meeting">
    Navigate to **Meetings** and select a meeting whose agenda structure you want to reuse.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the agenda">
    Click the **Agenda** tab and confirm the items are in the state you want to template.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save as template">
    Click the **More options (⋯)** menu in the top-right of the Agenda tab, then select **Save as Template**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name the template">
    Enter a descriptive name (e.g., "Monthly City Council — Regular Session") and choose whether to save it as a personal or organization-level template.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review and save">
    Confirm the item list, adjust any placeholder descriptions, and click **Save Template**. The template is now available for future meetings.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Applying a template

When creating a new meeting, look for the **Apply Template** option on the meeting creation form. Select a template from the list and click **Apply**. ConnectMeetings pre-populates the agenda with all items from the template. You can then edit, reorder, or remove items before saving.

You can also apply a template to an existing meeting with an empty agenda. Open the **Agenda** tab, click **Apply Template**, and choose the template you want to use.

<Tip>
Templates are an easy way to enforce compliance in formal governance settings. Include required items — such as "Disclosure of Conflicts of Interest" or "Public Comment Period" — in every relevant template so they are never accidentally omitted from an agenda.
</Tip>

## Editing template items

Changes to a template affect only future meetings where the template is applied. Meetings that were already created using the template are not changed — their agendas are independent copies.

To edit a template, go to **Settings → Templates**, select the template, make your changes, and click **Save**. Add a note in the template description to communicate what changed, especially for shared organization-level templates.

## Organization-level vs. personal templates

ConnectMeetings supports two levels of template ownership:

| Type | Who can create | Who can use | Who can edit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Organization** | Admins | All organizers in the org | Admins only |
| **Personal** | Any organizer | That organizer only | That organizer only |

Organization-level templates are ideal for standardizing formats across departments or committees. Personal templates suit organizers who run specialized meetings with a structure unique to their role.
